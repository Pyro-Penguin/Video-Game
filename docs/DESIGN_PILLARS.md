# Design Pillars

**Status:** Provisional principles inferred from current concept notes.

## 1. The Player Is Part of the Team
The player character should matter mechanically, not merely issue commands. Class/build, equipment, and survival can be central to battle outcomes.

## 2. Monsters Are Partners
Support collection, discovery, individual identity, and meaningful roster decisions. A lead companion may visibly travel with the player; a second companion may optionally accompany them.

## 3. One Ability System, Two Contexts
Abilities should ideally have combat and world uses. A spell that affects enemies or battlefields may also move a pillar, freeze water, clear growth, or reveal a route.

## 4. Exploration Rewards Curiosity
Dungeons should behave like physical puzzle spaces. Acquired abilities and tools can open new routes and reward backtracking.

## 5. Competitive Rules Must Be Legible
PvP rules should be deterministic where possible, clearly communicated, and independently testable. RNG, type relationships, and action resolution should be measured rather than selected by intuition.

## 6. The World Reacts
Recurring rivals, tournament participation, spectators, and NPC reactions can provide continuity. A nemesis-style system is a candidate, not a committed feature.

## 7. Visual and Audio Cohesion
Expressive, atmospheric, whimsical exploration contrasts with high-energy combat. Transitions should feel seamless.

## 8. Scope Discipline
Validate the battle core before producing a large roster, full world, elaborate cinematics, or online systems.
